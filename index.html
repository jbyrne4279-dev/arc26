Why it exists

Willpower isn't a plan.

Most days you already know what to do and skip it anyway — the phone is right there and motivation runs out fast. Winter Arc 26 replaces willpower with structure: tasks you have to prove, a lock that holds until they're done, and a score that remembers whether you showed up.

The daily loop

How a day works

The same short loop every day, resetting at 5am.

Step 01

Set your tasks

A handful of daily tasks you choose during onboarding and can edit anytime. Each one is photo-verified, tied to a lock session, or a simple check-off.

Step 02

Lock in

Start a session and the apps you picked are shielded by Screen Time. Open one and you get a block screen instead. The shield stays up until every task is done.

Step 03

Settle the score

At the 5am rollover the day is graded. A full clear adds ELO and extends your streak; a missed day costs you. Your rank moves to match.

Features

What Winter Arc actually does.

A handful of systems that make discipline measurable. Here's each one — and what it looks like in the app.

Verified tasks

Tasks you prove, not just tick.

For most tasks, tapping "done" isn't an option. You open the camera live, take the shot, and it's checked against that task's own criteria — coming back verified or rejected, usually within a second or two.

  • Live capture only — the camera roll is disabled, so old photos won't pass.
  • Each task is judged against its own accept and reject criteria.
  • The photo is used for the check, then discarded — never saved or posted.
Lock Mode

A lock that holds until the work is done.

Start a session and Winter Arc uses Screen Time to shield the apps you chose. It's tied to your task list, not a countdown — the shield lifts only when every task for the day is complete.

  • Task-bound: finish your tasks to unlock, don't wait out a timer.
  • Opening a blocked app shows a full-screen block, not the app.
  • Banked reward minutes buy a short break, then the shield returns on its own.
The exit gate

Quitting early means making your case.

There's no one-tap escape mid-session. Tap to end and a coach challenges the decision across a few rounds; the real "end session" button only appears after it delivers its verdict.

  • Five back-and-forth turns before any exit is shown.
  • Built to make you argue with yourself, not the app.
  • If the network drops, it falls back to a script — you're never locked out by an error.
Earn time back

Win a game, bank fifteen minutes.

Six short brain games — Mental Math, Focus, Memory, Attention, N-Back and Spatial. Clear one and you bank 15 minutes of reward time to spend during a locked session.

  • Six games, each procedurally generated — nothing to memorise.
  • One win a day banks minutes; spend them in 15-minute blocks.
  • Reward time is capped, so a break stays a break.
Daily rewards

Three tasks done mints a crate.

Clear three tasks in a day and you earn that day's crate. Open it for a reward — often more banked minutes — so consistent days quietly stack up small wins.

  • Earned crates are saved — you can't lose one by closing the app.
  • Rewards feed back into your minute balance for lock sessions.
  • A small, daily reason to get the first three done.
Also in the app

And a few more things.

Streak shields

Hit a 30-day streak and earn a shield, holding up to two. Miss a day and the first slip is forgiven; a second miss in a row spends a shield instead of breaking your streak.

Morning briefing

On the first open each day, a set of swipeable cards grades yesterday by how much you finished and sets up the day ahead. It doesn't sugar-coat a bad one.

Community

Post Wins, Struggles and Questions under a username you pick, react to others, and see you're not the only one grinding. Everything is moderated automatically.

Custom, flexible tasks

Mix prebuilt tasks with your own check-off habits, and edit the list whenever your routine changes. The daily loop bends to your goals, not the other way round.
